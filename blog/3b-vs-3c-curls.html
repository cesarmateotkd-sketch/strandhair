3B vs 3C Curls: What's the Difference and How to Care for Each

By Strand Editorial  ·  June 2025  ·  8 min read

If you know you have Type 3 curly hair but can't figure out whether you're a 3B or a 3C — you're not alone. These two subtypes look similar at a glance and share many of the same care needs, but they differ enough that the wrong products can leave your curls either weighed down and limp (if you're a 3B using 3C products) or frizzy and dry (if you're a 3C using 3B products).

This guide breaks down exactly how to tell the difference, what those differences mean for your routine, and the best products for each type — ordered by how you'd use them on wash day.

The Quick Answer: How to Tell 3B from 3C

The simplest way to identify your curl type is to look at a single, freshly washed curl on completely dry hair with no product in it. Compare the width of the curl to a common object:

  • 3B: Your curls are roughly the width of a marker, your finger, or a thick Sharpie. The curl is a defined, springy corkscrew or spiral. It has real bounce — if you stretch one curl and let go, it springs back visibly.
  • 3C: Your curls are tighter — closer to the width of a pencil, a straw, or a crochet needle. They're packed more densely on your head, creating more volume overall. There's more shrinkage than 3B — your curls might shorten by 30–50% when dry.

Mixed types are very common: Many people have 3B curls at the crown and 3C curls at the nape, or vice versa. If this is you, use the products for the tighter pattern — products for tighter curl types will work on looser areas, but not the other way around.

Type

3B Curls

  • Marker or finger width spirals
  • Defined, springy corkscrews
  • Medium density and volume
  • 30–40% shrinkage when dry
  • Prone to frizz, especially in humidity
  • Needs moisture but can be weighed down by heavy products
  • Often shiny when healthy
Type

3C Curls

  • Pencil or straw width corkscrews
  • Tighter, denser, more packed
  • High volume and density
  • 40–50% shrinkage when dry
  • More fragile than 3B — more prone to dryness
  • Needs richer moisture without being heavy
  • Sits closer to Type 4 in care needs

The Key Product Difference Between 3B and 3C

Both types need sulfate-free cleansing, regular deep conditioning, and a leave-in-plus-styler combination. The main difference is product richness:

  • 3B hair typically does well with medium-weight products. A lightweight leave-in conditioner plus a curl cream or medium-hold gel is often the sweet spot. Heavy butters and oils can weigh 3B curls down and cause them to lose definition.
  • 3C hair needs richer products than 3B because the tighter pattern makes it harder for scalp oils to travel down each strand. A more moisturizing leave-in, a richer curl cream, and a stronger gel will generally give 3C hair the hydration and hold it needs.

The 3B Wash Day Routine

Step 1: Co-Wash or Sulfate-Free Shampoo

3B hair benefits from co-washing 1–2 times per week to preserve moisture, with a light clarifying shampoo used once every 2–3 weeks to remove buildup. If your scalp gets oily quickly, lean toward shampoo more often.

The 3C Wash Day Routine

3C hair follows a very similar routine to 3B but with richer products at each step and a greater emphasis on deep conditioning. The routine below reflects the upgrade in product richness that tighter 3C curls typically need.

What 3B and 3C Have in Common

Despite their differences, both types share the same foundational care rules:

  • Deep condition every single week — this is not optional for Type 3 hair
  • Apply all products to soaking wet hair, never damp or dry
  • Diffuse on low heat or air dry — never rub with a terry cloth towel
  • Detangle only with fingers or a wide-tooth comb on wet, conditioner-saturated hair
  • Protect your curls at night with a satin bonnet or silk pillowcase
  • Scrunch out the crunch once hair is completely dry — never while wet
