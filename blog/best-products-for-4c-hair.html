The Best Products for 4C Hair, Step by Step

June 2025  ·  Updated November 2026  ·  10 min read

CBy César, founder of Strand · studies linked in the text

4C hair tends to feel dry at the ends and knots easily: tightly coiled hair formed knots in 10 to 16% of hair shafts in one microscopy study (J Am Acad Dermatol 2000). So the routine that works is water-based moisture, sealed in, plus gentle handling. (You’ll often read that scalp oil can’t travel down a tight coil; it’s widely repeated but hasn’t been studied.)

The products listed here are ordered by routine step — from pre-poo to sealing oil — so you know exactly what to use, when, and why. No paid placements, no brand deals.

What 4C Hair Actually Needs

Before we get into specific products, here's the logic that should guide every purchase decision for 4C hair:

  • Moisture, layered: water is what moisturizes; oil slows it leaving. An oil film reduced how much moisture hair absorbed in a lab study (J Cosmet Sci 2007), so water-based products go on first.
  • The LOC or LCO method: Liquid (leave-in) → Oil → Cream is the most common layering order for 4C coils. No study compares it with LCO; both put water first and oil after. (More on this in our LOC Method guide.)
  • Gentle cleansing every 1–2 weeks: the American Academy of Dermatology suggests washing tightly coiled hair once a week or every other week. Clarify now and then if products build up.
  • Weekly deep conditioning with warmth: a shower cap plus warm towel works. In lab tests, conditioning ingredients diffused into hair faster as temperature rose (Int J Cosmet Sci 2004).
  • Gentle handling always: Detangle only on wet, product-saturated hair. Never dry. Wide-tooth comb or fingers only.

The Complete 4C Routine at a Glance

  1. Pre-Poo: Apply coconut oil (the best evidence), castor or rosemary mint oil to dry hair, leave 15–30 min
  2. Shampoo: Cleanse in sections every 1–2 weeks with a sulfate-free formula
  3. Deep Condition: Weekly, with heat, 15–30 minutes
  4. Leave-In: Apply generously to soaking wet hair (the L in LOC)
  5. Cream: Layer on top of leave-in for moisture depth (the C in LOC)
  6. Sealing Oil: Lock everything in, especially at ends (the O in LOC)

Frequently Asked Questions

What products do you need for 4C hair?

A gentle shampoo, a moisturizing deep conditioner, a water-based leave-in, a light oil (such as jojoba or argan) and a cream or butter to seal. Add a clarifying shampoo now and then, and a protein treatment if your hair is damaged.

What is the best moisturizer for 4C hair?

Water is the best moisturizer for 4C hair, so look for leave-ins with water or aloe vera listed first. The LOC method (leave-in, oil, cream) layers moisture and seals it in; an oil film slows how fast water leaves hair. Popular picks include As I Am Leave-In Conditioner, SheaMoisture Manuka Honey leave-in and Kinky-Curly Knot Today.

Should 4C hair use protein or moisture?

Both, depending on damage. Protein treatments reinforced damaged hair fibers in lab tests, so they help most on hair that has been colored, heat-styled or roughly handled. Signs you need protein: hair stretches without bouncing back and feels mushy when wet. Signs you need moisture: hair snaps when stretched even slightly. No study has set a schedule; a light protein product every 4–6 weeks is a common starting point.

Is coconut oil good for 4C hair?

Usually, yes. Coconut oil is one of the few oils shown to get into the hair shaft, and used before washing it reduced protein loss in lab tests. Some people find it leaves their hair stiff; if that happens, try olive oil, which also got into hair in lab tests. Use it as a pre-poo or in the oil step of LOC.

How often should I moisturize 4C hair?

Most 4C routines refresh moisture every 2–3 days: water or a water-based spray first, then a light oil or cream. On wash day, use the full LOC method. Avoid sealing dry hair; oil on dry hair mostly keeps water out.

What ingredients should I avoid in 4C hair products?

Strong sulfate shampoos can leave coily hair feeling dry, so save them for occasional clarifying. Mineral oil and petrolatum stay on the surface, so don’t rely on them as your only moisturizer. Avoid drying alcohols (isopropyl, SD alcohol) in leave-ins. Heavier silicones can build up; clarify now and then if you use them.
