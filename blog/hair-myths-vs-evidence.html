Natural Hair Myths vs Evidence: 36 Claims Checked

November 2026 · Updated November 25 · 15 min read

CBy César, founder of Strand · studies linked in the text

Most natural hair advice has never been tested. We checked 36 of the most repeated claims about growth, oils, porosity, washing, heat and protective styles against published studies. Each one gets a verdict and a link to the study behind it, so you can read the source yourself.

20Not supported
9Partly true / limited
5Supported
2Not studied

How to read the verdicts. Not supported means studies contradict the claim. Partly true means the evidence is small, mixed or depends on the situation. Supported means at least one controlled study backs it, often a lab study on hair fibers. Not studied means no one has tested it. Lab results on hair fibers don't always carry over to a full head of hair, and we say so where it matters.

Hair growth myths

MythWhat research showsVerdict
Type 4 hair grows slower, or doesn't grow.Scalp hair grew roughly 0.3 to 0.5 inches a month in regrowth studies, with a wide range between people. No study has measured growth by curl type. Hair grows every month; breakage is why length doesn't show. (Br J Dermatol 2001, Eur J Dermatol 2016)How to keep 4C length →Not supported
If your hair never gets longer, it isn't growing.People with tightly coiled hair who combed it daily still had short hair years after a haircut (about 4 to 7 cm). The hair grew; it broke off. (J Am Acad Dermatol 2006)Detangling without breakage →Not supported
Castor oil makes hair grow.No study has tested castor oil on its own for hair growth in people. A 2026 dermatology review called its proposed growth mechanism unproven in practice. (Cureus 2026)Castor oil: what it can do →Not supported
Rosemary oil works as well as minoxidil.In one randomized trial of 100 people with pattern hair loss, rosemary oil matched 2% minoxidil for hair count after 6 months. There was no placebo group, and it was not a trial on breakage or curl type. (Skinmed 2015)Scalp care routine →Limited evidence
Peppermint oil grows hair.Peppermint oil thickened follicles in mice. It has not been tested on people's scalps. (Toxicol Res 2014)Not studied in people
Scalp massage does nothing.In a small study, 4 minutes of daily standardized scalp massage for 24 weeks increased hair thickness in 9 men. Small, with no control group. (Eplasty 2016)Limited evidence

Oils myths

MythWhat research showsVerdict
All oils soak into hair.Coconut oil penetrates the hair shaft and mineral oil stays on the surface. Olive and sunflower oil also got in. Jojoba, avocado and almond oil are often called "penetrating" but haven't been tested. (J Cosmet Sci 2001, J Cosmet Sci 2005)Best oils for natural hair →Partly true
Oil moisturizes hair.An oil film reduced how much water vapor hair absorbed. Water is what moisturizes; oil slows it leaving (and entering). Apply water-based products first, oil last. (J Cosmet Sci 2007)The LOC method →Misleading
Pre-pooing is hype.Coconut oil applied before washing reduced protein loss from hair; mineral and sunflower oil did not. A 2022 study on damaged hair agreed. (J Cosmet Sci 2003, Int J Cosmet Sci 2022)Pre-poo guide →Supported (lab)
Oiling the scalp is good for every scalp.Dermatologists reported that hair oils may worsen seborrheic dermatitis. On a flaky, itchy, inflamed scalp, skip scalp oiling and see a dermatologist. (Skin Appendage Disord 2023)Scalp care routine →Not for every scalp
More castor oil is always better.Rare, but castor oil has been linked to sudden hair felting (matting that had to be cut out). Use small amounts on hair in sections. (Int J Trichology 2017)Use with care

Porosity myths

MythWhat research showsVerdict
The float test tells you your porosity.No at-home porosity test, the float test included, has been validated. What lab measurement shows is that porosity tracks damage: bleaching nearly tripled the hair surface water can reach. (J Cosmet Sci 2008)Porosity signs that work →Not supported
Porosity depends on your curl type.No study has measured porosity by curl type. Porosity rises with damage such as bleaching, so 3C or 4C hair can be low, normal or high. (J Cosmet Sci 2008)Low porosity routine →Not supported
Heat while deep conditioning makes no difference.Heat improved how much oil hair took up, and conditioning ingredients diffused into hair faster as temperature rose. (J Cosmet Sci 2005, Int J Cosmet Sci 2004)Deep conditioning →Supported (lab)

Washing myths

MythWhat research showsVerdict
Curly hair should be washed as rarely as possible.In a survey plus a controlled wash study, satisfaction with hair and scalp was highest with frequent washing, and frequent washing was not linked to worse hair (the study was not on curly or coily hair). The American Academy of Dermatology suggests weekly or every other week for tightly coiled hair. (Skin Appendage Disord 2021)4C wash day →Not supported
Co-washing can replace shampoo.No study compares co-wash and shampoo on curly or coily hair. Co-wash is too mild to remove heavy product and scalp oil on its own; keep a real shampoo in the routine. (Skin Appendage Disord 2021)Co-washing guide →Not studied
Sulfates damage hair, so always go sulfate-free.In a lab test, hair rubbed in a lauryl sulfate solution lost about 7 times more protein than hair rubbed in water (about twice as much when only soaked), and losses rose with heat and damage. Strong cleansers can also add static and frizz, which is why shampoos mix in milder surfactants and why conditioner follows. But no published study has compared sulfate-free and sulfate shampoos for cleaning power or hair damage, on curly hair or any other. A dermatology review still suggests a stronger shampoo every 10 or so washes to clear product buildup. (Colloids Surf B 2005, Int J Trichology 2015)Clarifying shampoos →Partly true
Shampoo pH doesn't matter.Alkaline (high-pH) shampoos increased friction between fibers in the lab, which means more tangles. Apple cider vinegar rinses themselves haven't been studied. (Int J Trichology 2014)ACV rinse →pH matters (lab)

Detangling and breakage myths

MythWhat research showsVerdict
Detangling daily keeps tangles away.Surface damage rose steadily with the number of combing strokes on tightly coiled hair, and 480 strokes did more cuticle damage than frequent braiding. Detangle on wash day, wet, with slip. (Int J Cosmet Sci 2021, J Am Acad Dermatol 2006)Detangling guide →Not supported
Coily hair breaks because its chemistry is different.Researchers linked breakage in curly hair to its shape plus grooming stress, not different chemistry. Tightly coiled hair forms knots: 10 to 16% of hair shafts in one microscopy study. (Br J Dermatol 2015, J Am Acad Dermatol 2000)Breakage vs shedding →Not supported
Conditioner just sits on the surface and does nothing.Conditioners with protein, panthenol or silicones reduced protein loss from dyed hair. Damaged hair benefits most. (J Cosmet Dermatol 2018)Leave-ins for 4C →Not supported

Heat myths

MythWhat research showsVerdict
Heat damage washes out.In a controlled flat-iron study, curly hair straightened permanently, and how much depended on temperature and number of passes. Curls that don't come back are heat damage. (J Cosmet Sci 2013)Silk press at home →Not supported
Hotter and faster is safer than more passes.Pores inside the hair rose rapidly between about 374 and 428°F (190–220°C). Stay under about 375°F with one slow pass per section. (J Cosmet Sci 2011)Heat protectants →Not supported
Flat-ironing slightly damp hair is fine.Ironing damp hair caused far more structural damage than ironing dry hair. Blow-dry fully and let any spray dry before the iron. (J Cosmet Sci 2011)Not supported
Heat protectant is a gimmick.Polymer pretreatments cut combing breakage after flat-ironing. They reduce damage; they don't make high heat safe. (J Cosmet Sci 2011)Heat protectants →Supported (lab)
Air-drying is always the gentle option.Surface damage rose with dryer temperature, but damage to the layer between hair cells appeared only in hair left to dry naturally. Low heat with a diffuser is a reasonable trade-off. (Ann Dermatol 2011)Twist-out guide →Partly true

Protective styles myths

MythWhat research showsVerdict
Protective styles can't hurt your hairline.Traction alopecia was found in 31.7% of adult women in a South African population study. Risk rises with how hard and how long a style pulls. (J Am Acad Dermatol 2008, Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol 2018)Protective styles →Not supported
Tight braids just need a few days to settle.Pain, bumps or tightness after an install signal higher traction risk. If it hurts, ask for it to be redone looser. (Dermatol Clin 2014)Not supported
Sewn-in weaves and extensions carry no scalp risk.Sewn-in weaves and braids with extensions were linked to central scarring hair loss (CCCA). It's an association, not proof of cause; watch for crown tenderness and see a dermatologist early. (J Am Acad Dermatol 2009)Some risk

Products and ingredients myths

MythWhat research showsVerdict
Glycerin always moisturizes.Glycerin holds water, but very concentrated glycerin pulls moisture from the air around it. That's why glycerin-heavy products can act differently by season. Not tested on hair. (Sci Rep 2022)Best gels →Depends
A good gel holds the same in any weather.Humid air softens styling polymers and weakens hold, and hold depends on both the polymer and the hair itself. (J Cosmet Sci 2009, J Cosmet Sci 2001)Best gels →Not supported
Bond builders are protein treatments.Bond builders work on broken disulfide bonds inside chemically damaged hair; protein treatments add proteins that reinforced damaged hair fibers in lab tests. Different jobs. (Int J Cosmet Sci 2024, Polymers 2023)Protein treatments →Not supported
Vitamin B5 (panthenol) can't get into hair.Panthenol penetrated hair and bound to its proteins in spectroscopy studies. (Int J Cosmet Sci 2026)Low porosity routine →Not supported
Tea tree oil for dandruff is folklore.A 5% tea tree oil shampoo improved dandruff by 41%, against 11% with placebo, in a randomized trial of 126 people. (J Am Acad Dermatol 2002)Scalp care routine →Supported (trial)
Silicones damage hair.Silicones are the most widely used conditioning ingredient. Dimethicone coats the hair to protect it from rubbing and friction, and silicone heat protectants spread heat along the strand. No study we found shows they damage hair. Silicones differ in how easily they rinse out, so heavy leave-in use can leave residue; a clarifying shampoo removes it. (Int J Trichology 2015)Heat protectants →Not supported

Hair typing myths

MythWhat research showsVerdict
Curl type charts are a scientific measurement.When trained raters sorted hair into 8 curl groups they often disagreed, and no volunteer's 10 sampled hairs all fell in the same group. 1A–4C is a useful shared language, not a lab measurement. (PLoS One 2017, J Cosmet Dermatol 2026)Find your hair type →Not supported

What the evidence adds up to

Across all 36 claims, one pattern stands out: hair grows every month; breakage is why length doesn’t show. The best-supported habits are about handling, not miracle ingredients: pre-poo with coconut oil, detangle less often and only with slip, keep heat low and hair dry before ironing, and keep styles loose. Growth oils, porosity tests and strict “never shampoo” rules have little or no support.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does any oil make hair grow faster?

No oil has been shown to speed up hair growth in people. Castor oil has never been tested alone for growth; rosemary oil matched 2% minoxidil in one trial of people with pattern hair loss, with no placebo group. What oils can do is reduce breakage, which helps you keep the length you grow.

Is the hair porosity float test accurate?

No. No at-home porosity test, the float test included, has been validated. Porosity tracks damage such as bleaching. Go by signs instead: how fast hair gets wet and dries, and whether products sit on top.

Do protective styles help hair grow?

Protective styles don't change how fast hair grows. They can reduce daily combing, which helps retention, but styles that pull too hard or stay in too long raise the risk of traction alopecia.

Does type 4 hair grow slower than other hair types?

No study has measured growth rate by curl type. Scalp hair grows roughly 0.3 to 0.5 inches a month, with a wide range between people. Hair grows every month; breakage is why length doesn't show.

How were these myths checked?

Each claim was checked against published studies on PubMed. Each verdict says how strong the evidence is: lab studies on hair fibers, small clinical studies, randomized trials, or not studied at all.
