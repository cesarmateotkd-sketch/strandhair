High Porosity Hair: Causes, Routine and Best Products

April 21, 2026  ·  10 min read

CBy César, founder of Strand · studies linked in the text

High porosity hair takes in water fast and loses it fast. It gets wet in seconds, dries quickly and frizzes easily, because the outer layer of the hair has gaps. In most cases that comes from damage: in a lab study, bleaching nearly tripled the hair surface that water can reach (J Cosmet Sci 2008). Color, heat and rough handling all add to it. The routine below slows moisture loss and protects the hair you have while damaged ends grow out.

High porosity hair has a damaged outer layer, so water gets in easily and leaves easily. The fix is not more hydration — it is learning to slow the loss.

Signs Your Hair Is High Porosity

Not sure if this is you? Take our hair porosity test for the full diagnostic, or check these behavioral signs:

Soaks Up Water Fast

Hair gets completely wet within seconds in the shower — no resistance at all.

Dries Quickly

Hair dries faster than expected for its density — sometimes within an hour.

Frizz in Humidity

Raised cuticles let moisture in and out freely, causing unpredictable frizz.

Products Disappear Fast

Anything you apply absorbs immediately — hair feels dry again within hours.

Tangles Easily

Raised cuticle scales snag on each other, creating tangles and breakage.

Color Fades Fast

High porosity hair loses color molecules faster than normal porosity.

Why High Porosity Hair Stays Dry

Think of low porosity hair as a sealed container and high porosity hair as a colander. The colander fills with water instantly — but just as quickly, the water runs straight through the holes. The goal of a high porosity routine is to patch those holes (protein) and cover them with a barrier (heavy sealants) so moisture cannot escape as fast as it entered.

Is high porosity genetic? Many sources say it can be, but no study has shown porosity to be inherited. What research does show is that damage raises porosity, so the best long-term fix is less damage: gentler detangling, less heat and color, and trimming damaged ends as they grow out.

The 5-Step High Porosity Routine

1

Pre-poo with a penetrating oil

Apply coconut oil to dry hair 30–60 minutes before washing. This step has some of the best lab evidence in hair care: coconut oil before washing reduced protein loss in both undamaged and damaged hair, while mineral and sunflower oil did not (J Cosmet Sci 2003), and a coconut-based oil reduced the damage shampoo surfactants do inside the hair (Int J Cosmet Sci 2022).

2

Protein treatment every 4–6 weeks

In lab studies on damaged hair, conditioners with protein, panthenol and silicone reduced protein loss from dyed hair (J Cosmet Dermatol 2018), and a keratin repair treatment reduced how fast damaged hair lost water (Polymers 2023). Use a light protein treatment (keratin, rice protein) monthly, and a heavy protein treatment (ApHogee Two-Step) every 3–4 months if hair is severely damaged. Follow protein with a moisturizing deep conditioner. No study sets how often to use protein, so let your hair guide you: stiff or straw-like means use less.

3

Deep condition with moisture-rich formula

High porosity hair can handle richer deep conditioners than low porosity. Look for shea butter, aloe vera, honey, and ceramides in the formula. Heat is optional: in lab studies, warmth increased how much got into hair (J Cosmet Sci 2005), so 20–30 minutes under a thermal cap helps. The popular cool rinse to “close the cuticle” hasn’t been tested; do it if you like the feel.

4

Apply LOC method with heavy products

High porosity hair usually does well with LOC (Liquid, Oil, Cream). The part with evidence is water first: an oil film reduced how much moisture hair absorbed (J Cosmet Sci 2007), so get water in before any oil. The oil-before-cream order is stylist practice, not studied. Most high porosity readers prefer richer products: a cream leave-in rather than a mist, castor oil or a butter cream to finish. Apply everything to damp hair immediately after washing — do not let hair start drying first.

5

Seal with a heavy oil or butter

The sealing step is non-negotiable for high porosity hair. Jamaican black castor oil, shea butter or mango butter sit on the surface and slow water loss for a while. How long they hold moisture in hasn’t been measured, so judge by how long your hair stays soft. Light oils like argan or jojoba seal too, just with less weight.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best conditioner for high porosity hair? +

For high porosity hair, alternate two kinds: a rich moisture conditioner most weeks and a protein treatment every 4–6 weeks. In a lab study (J Cosmet Dermatol 2018), conditioners with protein, panthenol or silicones reduced protein loss from damaged, dyed hair. Pick a rich, creamy formula, leave it on under warmth, and follow with a leave-in and a sealing oil. No study compares conditioner brands; the SheaMoisture Manuka Honey masque and ApHogee reconstructor in our list fill those two roles.

What causes high porosity hair? +

Mostly damage. Bleaching, color, relaxers, heat and rough detangling all open gaps in the hair’s outer layer; in one lab study, bleaching nearly tripled the hair surface water can reach. Some sources say high porosity can be genetic, but no study has shown that. Damaged hair doesn’t heal, so the fix is gentler care while damaged ends grow out and get trimmed.

How do I know if my hair is high porosity? +

Watch how your hair behaves: it gets wet almost instantly, dries fast, frizzes in humidity, tangles easily, and feels dry again a few hours after products. Colored or heat-styled hair is more likely to be high porosity. The float and strand tests are popular but have never been validated, so behavior is a better guide.

Should high porosity hair use protein treatments? +

Often, yes. In lab studies on damaged hair, protein-containing conditioners reduced protein loss and a keratin treatment reduced water loss. No study sets how often to use protein, so start light and watch your hair: if it turns stiff or straw-like, use less and add moisture.

Is glycerin good or bad for high porosity hair? +

It depends on the weather and how you use it. Glycerin is a humectant: it pulls water from the air, so it works well in humid weather and can leave hair feeling dry in very dry air. Use it in a leave-in and seal with a cream or oil on top.

What oils are best for high porosity hair? +

Coconut oil before washing has the best evidence: it reduced protein loss from damaged hair in lab tests. For sealing after your leave-in, heavier oils and butters like castor oil or shea butter feel best to most high porosity readers, though how long any oil holds moisture in hasn’t been measured. Put water on first: an oil film reduces how much moisture hair absorbs.

Does apple cider vinegar help high porosity hair? +

It can make hair feel smoother for a while. Acidic products lower the hair’s negative charge, which reduces friction and frizz. Dilute it (about 1 part ACV to 4 parts water), use it as a final rinse after conditioning, and don’t use it undiluted. It doesn’t repair damage.
