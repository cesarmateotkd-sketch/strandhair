Hair Type Chart: 1A to 4C

Hair types run from 1 (straight) to 4 (coily), and the letters A to C go from the loosest to the tightest pattern in each type. On this chart every strand is drawn the same length, so the tighter patterns hang shorter. That’s shrinkage, and the dotted line in each square shows the strand’s real length.

How to read your hair against the chart

Look at clean hair with no product in it, after it has dried on its own. Compare the strands at the back and the crown as well as the front, because the pattern often changes across one head. Pick the type that covers most of your hair.

If two squares both look like you, you’re probably both. Treat the tighter areas a little more gently: researchers linked breakage in curly hair to its shape plus grooming stress, and tightly coiled hair forms knots more often. What research says about hair typing

The chart describes curl shape; it doesn’t measure it. When trained raters sorted hair samples into 8 curl groups, they often disagreed, and none of the volunteers had all 10 of their sampled hairs fall into the same group (PLoS One, 2017). So 1A to 4C is a useful shared language for picking routines and products, not a lab test. More on this in Hair myths vs evidence.

Questions

What are the hair types from 1A to 4C?

Type 1 is straight, type 2 wavy, type 3 curly and type 4 coily. The letters A to C run from the loosest to the tightest pattern within each type, so 4C is the tightest coil on the chart.

Can you have more than one hair type?

Yes, and most people do. In a 2017 study, none of the volunteers had all 10 of their sampled hairs fall into the same curl group. Use the type that covers most of your head, and treat the tighter areas a little more gently.

Is the hair type chart scientific?

No. It describes curl shape, but it isn't a measurement: trained raters sorting hair into 8 curl groups often disagreed. It's a shared language for choosing routines and products, not a lab test.
