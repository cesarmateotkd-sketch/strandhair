Wash Day Checklist for Natural Hair

A good wash day runs in the same order every time: pre-poo, shampoo the scalp, detangle with conditioner in, deep condition with warmth, leave-in on soaking wet hair, then style, dry and protect overnight. The 4C Wash Day Checklist

For coily hair, 4A–4C. Full guide: the 4C hair care routine.

  1. Pre-Poo30 min to overnight before washing

    Coat dry hair in sections with coconut oil. Lab tests: coconut oil before washing reduced protein loss from hair; mineral oil did not.

  2. Section & Finger-DetangleWork in 4–8 sections

    Divide hair into twisted sections. Gently separate the big tangles with fingers only — never a comb on dry 4C hair. Keep sections twisted through the whole wash.

  3. Shampoo the ScalpEvery 1–2 weeks

    Sulfate-free shampoo massaged into the scalp with fingertips (not nails). Let the suds rinse through the length — that’s enough cleansing for the ends. One section at a time.

  4. Detangle with ConditionerEnds first, roots last

    Apply a slippery rinse-out conditioner, then detangle each section with a wide-tooth comb from the ends up. Combing damage adds up with every stroke, so fewer, gentler passes.

  5. Deep Condition — With Warmth15–30 min, every wash day

    Coat every strand. Cover with a cap and add warmth: a steamer, heat cap or warm towel over the cap. Lab tests: warmth helped conditioning ingredients get into hair.

  6. L — Liquid / Leave-InOn soaking wet hair

    Water-based leave-in conditioner applied generously in sections. This is the moisture every later step is designed to keep in.

  7. O — OilFocus on the ends

    Seal the leave-in with a little oil. Lab tests: an oil film slowed how much water hair took up, so oil goes after water, never before.

  8. C — Cream, Then StyleStyle while damp

    Layer a curl cream, then style: twists, braids, or gel for a wash-and-go. Applying gel? Hair should still be dripping. Hands off until fully dry.

  9. Protect OvernightEvery single night

    Silk or satin bonnet, scarf or pillowcase. Satin is smoother than cotton, so there’s less rubbing against the pillow.

The rules that make it work

— Water first, oil last: oil or cream on dry hair keeps water out.

— Detangle only with slip — conditioner in, ends first, fingers before tools.

— Once a month, swap the deep conditioner for a protein treatment if hair feels overly stretchy or limp.

— Mid-week refresh: spray water + a little leave-in, re-seal ends with oil. No full re-wash needed.

The Waves & Curls Wash Day Checklist

For wavy and curly hair, 2A–3C. Full guides: wavy hair routine and 3C routine.

  1. Pre-Poo (Optional)Dry or damaged ends

    Coconut oil on the lengths 30 minutes before washing. Lab tests: coconut oil before washing reduced protein loss from hair; mineral oil did not.

  2. Shampoo the Scalp1–3× a week

    pH-balanced shampoo on the scalp only; let the suds rinse through. Lab tests: high-pH shampoos raised friction between hairs. Waves: a real shampoo at least weekly, since co-wash alone weighs waves down.

  3. ClarifyEvery 2–4 weeks

    Use a clarifying shampoo when products stop working or hair feels coated. Low porosity hair needs it most.

  4. Condition & DetangleEnds first, roots last

    Conditioner on mid-lengths and ends, then fingers and a wide-tooth comb while it’s in. Combing damage adds up with every stroke, so fewer, gentler passes. Skip brushing dry curls.

  5. Deep ConditionWhen hair feels dry

    15–20 minutes under a warm cap or towel. Lab tests: warmth helped conditioning ingredients get into hair. Limp or overly stretchy when wet? Use a protein treatment instead.

  6. Leave-InOn soaking wet hair

    Apply in the shower if you can. Light for waves, creamier for 3B–3C curls.

  7. StylerGel or mousse to hold

    Cream if you need moisture, then gel (curls) or mousse (waves). Scrunch, rake or brush-style into clumps. Oil, if any, last and light: an oil film slowed how much water hair took up.

  8. Dry, Hands OffLow heat or air dry

    Scrunch water out with a cotton T-shirt or microfiber towel (stylist practice). Diffuse on low or air dry: dryer heat raised surface damage, but long wet time had costs too. Scrunch out the cast once fully dry.

  9. Protect & RefreshEvery night

    Pineapple or loose bun with a satin bonnet or pillowcase. Day 2–3: mist with water, add a little styler, scrunch.

The rules that make it work

— Water first, oil last.

— Detangle only with slip — conditioner in, ends first.

— Change one product at a time, and give it 2–3 wash days before you judge it.

— Porosity matters more than curl type: no study has measured porosity by curl type, so 3A can be low or high.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I have a wash day?

For tightly coiled hair, the American Academy of Dermatology suggests washing once a week or every other week. Wavy hair usually needs washing more often, often 2 to 3 times a week, because it looks oily and weighed down sooner. Adjust to your scalp: itch and buildup mean wash more often.

Do I need to deep condition every wash day?

For 4C hair, most routines deep condition every wash day, and warmth helps conditioning ingredients get into hair in lab tests. Wavy and curly hair often only needs it when hair feels dry or rough.

Should I use the LOC or LCO method?

No study compares the two orders. LOC often suits hair that dries out fast, LCO hair where products sit on top. Both put water first and oil after it, which is the part with lab support.

What order should wash day steps go in?

Pre-poo, shampoo the scalp, detangle with conditioner in, deep condition with warmth, leave-in on soaking wet hair, then style, dry and protect overnight.
